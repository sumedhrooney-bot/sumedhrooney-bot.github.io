System Architecture & Theoretical Formulation

This project formulates an analytical framework linking forward-looking reference points ("aspirations") to dynamic capital allocation and investment decisions[cite: 6]. Grounded in the reference-dependent utility models of Ray and Genicot, the model investigates how a scalar aspiration target alters investment effort under a linear $AK$ (linear capital production where output is proportional to capital stock) production kernel[cite: 6]:

$$y = c + k, \quad k \ge 0, \quad f(k) = \rho k = z, \quad \text{with } \rho > 1$$

A household with current income $y$ allocates resources between consumption $c$ and investment $k$[cite: 6]. Investing $k$ yields return $z = \rho k$ in the subsequent period[cite: 6]. Preferences are characterized by Constant Relative Risk Aversion (CRRA) utility integrated with an asymmetric gain-loss penalty operator defined around an exogenous aspiration scalar $a$[cite: 6]:

$$U(c, k, a) = u(c) + \delta \left[ w_0(f(k)) + \pi_G W_G\Big((f(k) - a)_+\Big) - \pi_L W_L\Big((a - f(k))_+\Big) \right]$$

where $(x)_+ = \max\{x, 0\}$[cite: 6]. We assume identical Constant Relative Risk Aversion (CRRA) elasticity across all sub-utility components:

$$u(c) = c^{1-\sigma}, \quad w_0(z) = \delta z^{1-\sigma}, \quad W_G(e) = \delta\pi_G e^{1-\sigma}, \quad W_L(e) = \delta\pi_L e^{1-\sigma}$$

Here, $\sigma \in (0, 1)$ parameterizes the utility curvature, $\delta > 0$ represents the intertemporal discount weight, and $\pi_L > \pi_G > 0$ encodes empirical loss aversion (the psychic cost of failing to achieve the target exceeds the utility surplus of outperforming it)[cite: 6, 7].

To eliminate scale dependence, decision variables are normalized into scale-invariant ratios: endogenous growth factor $g = z / y$, aspiration ratio $r = a / y$, and investment share $i = k / y = g / \rho$[cite: 7]. This reduces the problem to maximizing a piecewise concave objective function[cite: 7]:

$$\tilde{U}(g, r) = \left(1 - \frac{g}{\rho}\right)^{1-\sigma} + \delta \left[ g^{1-\sigma} + \pi_G (g - r)_+^{1-\sigma} - \pi_L (r - g)_+^{1-\sigma} \right], \quad g \in [0, \rho]$$

Analytical Regimes & Structural Bifurcation

First-order optimization conditions ($FOCs$) partition the state space into two distinct behavioural regimes around a critical structural threshold $r^* > 1$[cite: 7]:

  • Satisfied Regime ($g \ge r$, for $r \le r^*$): The aspiration target is mathematically attainable within the household's budget envelope[cite: 7]. The optimal growth rate solves the gain-domain condition[cite: 7]:
    $$\left(1 - \frac{g}{\rho}\right)^{-\sigma} = \delta\rho \left[ g^{-\sigma} + \pi_G (g - r)^{-\sigma} \right]$$
    Differentiating with respect to $r$ gives $\frac{\partial g^*}{\partial r} > 0$[cite: 7]. Raising aspirations increases the marginal value of future capital, incentivizing higher investment shares[cite: 7].
  • Frustrated Regime ($g < r$, for $r > r^*$): When the aspiration ratio exceeds threshold $r^*$, the target cannot be reached without an unacceptable penalty to baseline consumption[cite: 7]. The loss penalty dominates, solving the condition[cite: 7]:
    $$\left(1 - \frac{g}{\rho}\right)^{-\sigma} = \delta\rho \left[ g^{-\sigma} + \pi_L (r - g)^{-\sigma} \right]$$
    Here, $\frac{\partial g^*}{\partial r} < 0$[cite: 7]. Unattainable targets induce an abandonment effect, depressing marginal investment incentives and producing a global inverted-U relationship between aspirations and capital accumulation[cite: 7]:
    $$k^*(y, a) = \frac{y}{\rho} g^*\left(\frac{a}{y}\right), \quad i^*(r) = \frac{g^*(r)}{\rho}$$

Data Engineering & Econometric Identification

To test whether this non-linear optimization mechanism manifests empirically, the structural model was evaluated using longitudinal microdata from the Indonesian Family Life Survey (IFLS, $N = 4,651$)[cite: 4, 8]. The dependent variable measures educational human capital accumulation, defined as $100 \times \ln(\text{Education Expenditure per capita})$[cite: 8].

To isolate causal elasticity and absorb unobserved local infrastructure, economic shocks, and community-level preferences, the econometric pipeline implemented quadratic polynomial regressions with high-dimensional Kecamatan (sub-district) fixed effects:

$$\ln(\text{Education\_Expenditure}_i) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 r_i + \beta_2 r_i^2 + \Gamma' X_i + \alpha_{\text{kecamatan}} + \varepsilon_i$$
