System Architecture & Theoretical Formulation

This research formulates an analytical framework linking bounded rationality, target-dependent loss functions, and resource allocation. Grounded in reference-dependent utility theory (Genicot & Ray), the project models how agent-selected scalar targets alter convex investment trajectories under an AK capital transition kernel ($f(k) = \rho k$, with $\rho > 1$).

The objective function combines a standard CRRA utility core over immediate consumption with an asymmetric penalty/reward operator referenced against a forward target $a$:

U(c, k, a) = u(c) + δ [ w0(f(k)) + π_G * max(0, f(k) - a)^(1 - σ) - π_L * max(0, a - f(k))^(1 - σ) ]

With parameter constraints $\sigma \in (0,1)$ defining constant relative risk aversion curvature and $\pi_L > \pi_G > 0$ imposing empirical loss aversion. By normalizing decision variables into scale-invariant ratios—endogenous growth factor $g = z/y$, aspiration ratio $r = a/y$, and investment rate $i = k/y = g/\rho$—the problem reduces to maximizing a piecewise concave objective function:

U(g, r) = (1 - g/ρ)^(1 - σ) + δ [ g^(1 - σ) + π_G * (g - r)_+^(1 - σ) - π_L * (r - g)_+^(1 - σ) ]

Analytical Regimes & Structural Bifurcation

First-order optimization conditions yield a dual-regime system governed by a structural bifurcation boundary $r^* > 1$:

  • Satisfied Regime ($g \ge r$, $r \le r^*$): The target is mathematically accessible within the agent's budget envelope. The first-order condition evaluates along the positive gain domain:
    (1 - g/ρ)^(-σ) = δρ [ g^(-σ) + π_G * (g - r)^(-σ) ]
    Here, $\partial g^* / \partial r > 0$. Increasing the target acts as an incentive mechanism, driving marginal investment up.
  • Frustrated Regime ($g < r$, $r > r^*$): When the target ratio exceeds critical threshold $r^*$, the agent cannot achieve the target without unsustainable sacrifice of base consumption. The loss penalty dominates:
    (1 - g/ρ)^(-σ) = δρ [ g^(-σ) + π_L * (r - g)^(-σ) ]
    Here, $\partial g^* / \partial r < 0$. Excessive target ratios trigger an analytical abandonment effect, depressing optimal investment and yielding an inverted-U global trajectory.

Data Engineering & Econometric Identification

To evaluate whether this non-linear optimization mechanism manifests in real-world data, the model was tested against longitudinal household records from the Indonesian Family Life Survey (IFLS, $N = 4,651$). The pipeline mapped survey modules into structured feature vectors, computing per-capita educational expenditure and normalized household aspiration ratios.

To isolate causal elasticity and eliminate unobserved spatial/community heterogeneity, the estimation pipeline applied quadratic polynomial specifications combined with high-dimensional sub-district fixed effects:

Technical Takeaways & Quantitative Relevance

  • Statistical Significance: Across all model iterations, the linear coefficient remains positive and significant at $p < 0.01$ ($\beta_1 \approx +34.5$), while the quadratic penalty term remains strictly negative at $p < 0.05$ ($\beta_2 \approx -4.33$), validating the inverted-U topology.
  • Optimal Inflection Point: Solving the estimated gradient $\frac{\partial \ln(Y)}{\partial r} = \beta_1 + 2\beta_2 r = 0$ yields a maximal investment response at an aspiration ratio of $r^* = -\frac{\beta_1}{2\beta_2} \approx 3.98$ to $4.48$, mapping directly to the theoretical threshold boundary.
  • Industry Application: Demonstrates expertise in non-linear loss-function formulation, reference-dependent agent behavior, high-dimensional panel data wrangling, and cluster-robust causal inference applicable to risk analytics, credit scoring, and customer underwriting behavior.
