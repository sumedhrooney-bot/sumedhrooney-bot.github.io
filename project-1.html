Aspirations, Investment, and the Inverted-U

Master's Thesis | Alfred Weber Institute, Heidelberg University | November 2025
Behavioral Economics Reference-Dependent Preferences Loss Aversion Microeconometrics IFLS Microdata R / Stata

Overview

This thesis models and empirically tests how forward-looking reference points ("aspirations") influence household capital allocation and investment decisions[cite: 6]. Building on the foundational framework of Genicot and Ray (2017), the study analyzes how the gap between a household's current resources and its future target dictates economic effort[cite: 6, 7]. The model establishes a theoretical mechanism producing an inverted-U relationship between aspirations and investment, which is subsequently validated using microdata on educational expenditures[cite: 7, 8].

Theoretical Framework

Households allocate income between current consumption and investment under an AK production technology[cite: 6]. Preferences are characterized by Constant Relative Risk Aversion (CRRA) utility integrated with reference-dependent gains and losses relative to an exogenous aspiration scalar[cite: 6]:

U(c, k, a) = u(c) + δ [ w0(f(k)) + πG WG((f(k) - a)+) - πL WL((a - f(k))+) ]

Where πL > πG captures loss aversion from falling short of aspirations[cite: 6, 7]. Under ratio normalization, the household optimization problem partitions into two distinct behavioural regimes[cite: 7]:

  • Satisfied Regime (g ≥ r): When the aspiration ratio is within achievable bounds, increasing aspirations raises marginal utility and encourages investment effort (∂g/∂r > 0)[cite: 7].
  • Frustrated Regime (g < r): When aspirations exceed a critical threshold r*, the target becomes unattainable; further increases in aspirations induce frustration, depressing investment incentives (∂g/∂r < 0)[cite: 7].

Together, these regimes generate an inverted-U investment profile: moderate aspirations stimulate savings and human capital accumulation, whereas excessive aspiration gaps depress effort[cite: 7].

Empirical Strategy & Key Findings

The non-linear prediction was tested empirically using household survey records from the Indonesian Family Life Survey (IFLS), estimating the effect of the aspirations ratio on per-capita education expenditure[cite: 4, 8]:

  • Econometric Specification: Evaluated using quadratic specifications alongside demographic controls, household asset levels, parental education, and kecamatan (sub-district) fixed effects with standard errors clustered at the enumeration area level[cite: 8].
  • Empirical Confirmation: Across all model specifications, the linear aspirations ratio enters with a positive coefficient (β ≈ +34.5 to +35.4, p < 0.01), while the quadratic term is negative and statistically significant (β ≈ -3.95 to -4.33, p < 0.05)[cite: 8].
  • Conclusion: Household educational investment responds non-monotonically to aspiration gaps, providing direct empirical evidence for the inverted-U hypothesis in developing economies[cite: 7, 8].
